      Design notes

      Design rationaleNotes for all six
      Design notes for this pagekept from the kit · departures

      Kept from the Electric Foundation kit

      1. The kit's web frame, unchanged: page #101013, card #1A1A1E, nested #1F1F24 (light: #F7F7F9 / #FFFFFF / #F1F1F4), and 328px market cards with radius 9 and 12px padding in a 4-column grid at 1440 with a 40px gutter.
      2. The kit's components: pill search, the category row with arrows, Yes/No outcome rows, full-width Up/Down and three-way buttons, and the bet slip with two price tiles, a Bold 32 amount, +$ chips and Confirm with ✕.
      3. The kit's card footer: "$Vol • date • category" with the gift and bookmark icons.
      4. Mono spec labels for machine facts: type, chain, collateral, market number, snapshot time.

      Deliberate departures

      1. Seer's Etherscan, across its interfaces. One explorer for every Seer market, with an Interface facet and column (Seer app, Futarchy, Deep, Risk Pricing, Opportunity) read from each market's own fields; where they can't tell, it says "Not identified".
      2. Two sides, one switch. Seer is a two-sided marketplace, so the explorer reads every market both ways: for information seekers it leads with what the market says and how much liquidity stands behind it; for traders, with prices, volume and dates.
      3. Discover, understand, trade. A market's title opens its detail: who is asking (and what else they ask), what it prices, how to use the signal, then a seekers' panel beside a traders' panel and "Trade on Seer". Trading itself stays on app.seer.pm.
      4. Explorer depth the kit doesn't draw. An Etherscan-style record per market (contract, condition ID, Reality.eth question, outcome-token ERC-20s, event log, positions, resolution) and per address, built only from kit surfaces; the sample walkthrough shows the full record.
      5. The dot grid carries data. In the lifecycle field each column is a market and each row a step; settled steps turn from dots into crosses, so the field reads as uncertainty becoming structure.
      6. State and verification on every card. An explorer has to say where a market is in its life. Amber (waiting) and red (dispute) only ever appear next to their label.
      7. Row buttons follow the market type. The kit shows Yes/No on every row. Here a categorical No buys the other outcomes as one basket; a scalar round trades Up/Down; multi-winner markets trade Buy/Sell, because a "No" there is not one clean bet.
      8. Each price in its market's unit. #2841 reads 8.14/10 · UP 81¢, #2842 reads 50% · 6.3¢ (a funded share pays 1/8 sDAI), #2850 a default chance per year. Charts, 24h moves and ticket payouts use the same unit.
      9. Kit fixes. Sample binary prices add up to 100¢ (the kit's 69¢ + 37¢ is 106¢); real markets keep Seer's own prices and say so on the card when Yes and No add up to more or less than 100¢. Amounts use a decimal point ("$100.00"), and markets use motif glyph tiles instead of photos.
      10. Type and contrast. PP Neue Montreal and Suisse Int'l Mono when installed, Geist and Geist Mono otherwise; muted text is lifted so 12px labels pass WCAG AA.
      About the data. Real: the question-asker block, "On Seer now", the Markets explorer with each market's detail, and the search's "On Seer · live" rows. They read Seer's public markets API live and fall back to a dated hourly snapshot, labelled as such; every filter but the market type is one of that API's own fields, and every trade link opens the market on app.seer.pm. The liquidity levels (under $1K low, $1K to $10K moderate, above that high) are our reading rule, not Seer's. Made up: the sample walkthrough and everything under it (activity, leaderboard, create), one shared sample dataset whose addresses all start with 0x5eed, so the same market shows up in Deep, Futarchy, Opportunity and Risk.
